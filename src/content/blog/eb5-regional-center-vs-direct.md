<intro>EB-5 regional center vs direct routes differ because USCIS allows up to 90 percent indirect jobs on regional center petitions filed on or after March 15, 2022. The decision is not simply "passive or active." The routes differ in how jobs may be counted, how the business is managed, and what records support the petition. The right choice depends on the specific business or project and the investor's experience, time, and risk tolerance.</intro>

## Job creation is a key difference

Both approaches require the investment to support the creation of qualifying jobs. USCIS generally requires at least 10 full-time jobs per investor. In a direct investment, the job plan generally relies on employees of the new commercial enterprise. Regional center investors may also rely on indirect jobs, using a reasonable methodology. Under current USCIS guidance, up to 90% of the required jobs for regional center petitions filed on or after March 15, 2022, may be indirect. <a href="https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-g-chapter-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USCIS Policy Manual: Job Creation</a>

The <a href="/research/post-reform-integrity-act-analysis">Reform and Integrity Act analysis</a> notes that job-counting distinction in brief. This article is the comparison of the two routes.

That difference can affect which kinds of businesses are practical. A business with a substantial qualifying payroll may support a direct investment. A larger development may rely on a broader economic analysis that estimates jobs connected to project spending. Ask how the project's <a href="/research/eb5-job-creation-report">job numbers</a> were calculated, what assumptions they rely on, and how employment will be documented.

## Decide how involved you want to be

Direct investment does not automatically mean the investor must personally run the business every day. The investor's role depends on how the enterprise is structured and managed. Some investors may be involved in operations. Others may invest in a business that has a separate management team.

Regional center projects are often structured so that an investor has a more limited role in day-to-day operations. That may appeal to someone who wants to keep running a business or working in a different field. But a limited management role does not remove the need for due diligence. You still need to understand the project, the people running it, how funds will be used, and what the offering documents say.

If you are considering a direct business, ask whether you have the time and experience to operate or oversee it. A franchise's name alone does not prove that a particular location is viable. Local demand, management, competition, costs, and the operator's experience all matter.

## Compare the work and documents

Ask what documents already exist and who is responsible for preparing them. A regional center project may include an economic analysis, business plan, offering documents, and project filings. A direct investment may require a detailed business plan and clear evidence that the enterprise can create and document the required jobs.

![Hands on a laptop with charts arranged across a desk](../../assets/eb5-route-comparison-charts.jpg)

USCIS reviews the investor's eligibility and petition evidence in either route. A project's approval does not guarantee that an individual investor's petition will be approved. Nor does an immigration approval guarantee that the business will perform well or that invested capital will be repaid.

Before committing funds, consider having an immigration attorney review the immigration structure and an independent financial professional assess the investment. These are separate reviews. Depending on the project, financial due diligence may include the capital stack, collateral, budget, timeline, management, and repayment terms. The site's <a href="/research/eb5-capital-stack">EB-5 capital stack guide</a> explains how project financing is layered.

## Match the route to your priorities

A direct investment may fit an entrepreneur who wants a role in a business and has relevant experience. It can also mean greater responsibility for business execution and employment records. A regional center investment may suit someone seeking a more hands-off role in a pooled project, but it also means relying on the project sponsor and management team.

Ask yourself how much control you want, how much time you can commit, and whether you understand the business or project. Then compare the job plan, documents, fees, and risks for the specific opportunities you are considering. There is no universal answer that applies to every investor.

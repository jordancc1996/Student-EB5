<intro>EB-5 job creation report totals should be read against the 10 full-time jobs USCIS requires for each investor. The projected job total is important, but it is not the whole analysis. Before investing, understand what the report counts, what assumptions support it, how jobs are allocated, and what could happen if the project falls behind.</intro>

USCIS requires an EB-5 investment to create at least 10 qualifying full-time jobs per investor. For regional center petitions, USCIS permits the use of reasonable methodologies to demonstrate indirect job creation, subject to statutory limits. The agency may also request evidence about how direct or indirect jobs are expected to be created. <a href="https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-g-chapter-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USCIS Policy Manual: Job Creation</a>

## Find out what the analysis includes

Ask whether the report counts direct, indirect, or induced jobs. Direct jobs are tied to an employer-employee relationship with the new commercial enterprise or, in some cases, the job-creating entity. Indirect jobs are created outside those entities as a result of the project. Induced jobs may result when workers spend their wages in the local economy.

![Printed page with a line chart on a stack of financial pages](../../assets/eb5-job-creation-chart.jpg)

Next, ask what expenditures or revenues drive the job estimate. Does the analysis rely on construction spending, ongoing operations, or both? Are the amounts in the report consistent with the project budget and business plan? Does the report explain which costs qualify and how the methodology connects those costs to job estimates?

The report should be reviewed by someone who can explain the assumptions and the model. If a major part of the job total depends on projected revenue or spending that has not yet occurred, ask how realistic those projections are and what evidence supports them.

## Test whether the project can carry out the plan

The project has to execute the activities behind the job estimate. Ask about permits, financing, construction status, contractor experience, and milestone dates. If construction was expected to begin by a certain date, has it? If costs rise or the timeline changes, what is the plan?

Financing is part of the picture. Ask whether the project depends on raising all anticipated EB-5 funds before it can proceed. Is there backup capital? Are bridge loans involved? What happens if the project's costs exceed its budget? These issues can affect the project's ability to move forward and the assumptions used to estimate job creation.

A government filing or project approval is not proof that all projected jobs have already been created. The project still needs to follow through on its business plan and generate the employment required.

## Understand how jobs are allocated

A project may forecast more jobs than the minimum, but each investor should understand the allocation rules. Review the offering documents to learn whether jobs are allocated by investment date, filing date, I-829 filing, or another method. Ask how many investors the project expects to accept and how the job calculation supports that number.

Ask the sponsor to explain the projected jobs per investor and any job cushion. A cushion means the project expects to create more jobs than the required minimum. It can provide some room if the project underperforms, but it is not a guarantee. The estimate still depends on the project's performance and the validity of its assumptions.

Also ask what happens if the project creates fewer jobs than expected. The offering documents should explain the allocation process and any relevant provisions. Do not assume that all investors will be treated identically if the project falls short. Job coverage and expected job creation also appear as interview questions in the <a href="/research/19-questions-rural-eb5-project-due-diligence">rural EB-5 due diligence questions</a>.

## Keep monitoring after you invest

Due diligence does not end when you file your petition. Read project updates and ask questions if construction, permits, financing, or other milestones fall behind. If the project's reported progress differs from its original timeline, ask for an explanation and understand how the change may affect the job analysis.

An immigration attorney can assess the immigration documents and job-creation plan. A financial professional can evaluate the project's budget, financing, collateral, and business risks. Those are different types of review. The <a href="/research/investment/what-to-know-before-investing-in-eb5">EB-5 pre-investment checklist</a> offers broader questions to consider, while the <a href="/research/eb5-capital-stack">capital stack guide</a> explains project financing.

A job-creation report is one part of project due diligence, not a guarantee of immigration approval or investment performance. Review it alongside the business plan, budget, offering documents, financing, and current project status. This article is general information, not legal or investment advice.
